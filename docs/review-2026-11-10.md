# Mission progress, graphics outlook, and where to rework  [FOR REVIEW, 2026-11-10]

Builder's review of the whole software as it stands at build 109, for David and Counsel, and the starting point for
the prompt to an outside analysis (Fable). Everything measured here was measured today or is cited from
`docs/dyno.md`; guesses are marked as guesses. Nothing in this document is decided: it's the material for deciding.

David's priority, in his words: attractive, honest, seamless far view graphics; "i believe the pyramid of cell scales
marched is probably the only right way to do it, but we have to implement the triangle renderer and raymarch renderer
in the most efficient way possible." And: "nothing is off the table."

---

## 1. The short version

- **Where it stands:** 20 days, 792 commits, build 108. About 62,000 lines of Rust and 5,100 of shader; 435 tests.
  The look, the world generation and the editor are in place and liked. The renderer is our own, on the card
  (wgpu, DirectX 12), with Bevy kept only for its scheduler and state.
- **How fast, on David's laptop** (RTX 5070 Ti Laptop, 2560 × 1600, the flights' settings, build 107/108):

  | View | Frames a second | The card's time |
  |---|---|---|
  | Flying the dyno's route (median) | about 215 | about 4.5 ms |
  | The route's slowest 1 in 100 | about 80 | about 12 ms |
  | Standing on the ground at the spawn | 113 to 150 | about 8 ms |
  | Beside a build of quarter blocks | about 70 | about 14 ms |

- **Against the spec** (David, 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-10): a 980 Ti desktop at 1080p, a smooth 60; David's laptop,
  a smooth 240 (at 2160p, or at its own 1600p: to be settled). Estimated (the 980 Ti not measured):
  - **The 980 Ti at 1080p:** about twice short in the worst views (near the ground, quarter-block builds).
  - **The laptop at 240, 1600p:** three to five times short in the worst views. **At 2160p:** six to ten times.
- **The verdict:** the far half of the renderer, the pyramid of cell scales marched, is the right design and
  should stay; nothing else gives an honest, seamless far view this cheaply. The structural costs are elsewhere, and
  each has a route:
  1. **The near field is drawn by rays.** Every near pixel walks blocks; every quarter block costs ray steps.
     Route: near chunks drawn as triangles, the march from where they end (section 6, route A).
  2. **Light is worked out by rays, every pixel, every frame** (the sun's and five sky rays). Near the ground it's
     half the frame. Route: light kept in the world, refreshed as the sun moves (route B).
  3. **The far march's blend is done in software,** twelve numbers from each of eight cells every step, with too
     many registers to keep the card busy. Route: lighter data and reuse between frames (route C).
  4. **The world's recipe on the card holds 255 registers** and runs at every block column of the whole map for the
     bake; and the rules exist in three copies (fractions, whole numbers, the card's). Route: one source, and a
     recipe shaped for the card (route E).

## 3. The graphics engine as built

### 3.1 The data: the form and its projection

The projection principle (`vision/projection.md`, "1 always equals 1") runs through everything:

```
the form (seed, maps, plans, the recipe in whole numbers)
   │  worked out per block column, the same on processor and card
   ▼
blocks ──► cells: each cell the exact sum of the blocks in it (solid, water, faces by way, colour, light)
   │
   ├─ live levels round the eye: cells of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 blocks, windows 672 cells a side, made tile by tile
   │  on the card as the eye moves (16 × 16 columns a tile; build 106: the finer ones take their ground
   │  from the coarsest's)
   └─ the bake: cells of 32 up to the whole map (73,728 blocks a side), 11.2 million cells, 180 MB; made once
      per world (about 8 minutes for a new world) and kept on disk (read back in 3 s)
```

**The store on the card** (`card_store.rs`): each cell column keeps only its **band**, the cells round its surface
(below: solid; above: air), in one pool (24 million cells, 384 MB allocated), found through two indexes (the live
windows, wrapping; the baked levels, whole-map). Compact (11 million cells for a whole world's far view), but a
column's cells aren't beside its neighbours' in memory, so the card's texture units can't blend them (section 5.6).

### 3.2 The frame

| Step | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Start pass | One cone per 8 × 8 tile finds where its rays can safely begin | `start_pass` |
| The pyramid | Layers of rays, coarse to fine (a ray per 8 × 8 pixels, then 4 × 4, then 2 × 2); a finer layer marches only where its parent says it's worth it, starting just short of its parent's hit | per layer |
| The march (`march_hit`) | **Near walk:** each ray walks blocks one by one to the face it meets (textures, quarter blocks by their octants, placed light). **Far march:** past where a block is a pixel, the ray marches the cells: skips air over column tops, strides, then fine steps through a surface, gathering colour from a smooth blend of eight cells (and two levels where one hands over to the next) | `march.wgsl` |
| Listing | Each layer's live pixels listed; the faces near the eye gathered into a table | `listed_staged` |
| Light per face | Each block face near the eye lit once at its middle: a sun ray and five sky rays | `face_light_staged` |
| Shading | Each other pixel's sun ray and sky rays (sky handed down from the parent where it matches) | `shade_staged` |
| Upscale | The layers brought up to the screen, writing the depth | `upscale.wgsl` |
| Objects | Models and sprites drawn as triangles into that depth; far lights' glows | `models.wgsl` |

### 3.3 Where the time goes (measured)

- **By pass** (GPU traces, build 104, shares of the card): over the land the march 32%, shading 29%, light per face
  19%, the near ground's tiles 15%; at sea the march 62%. **At the spawn, standing** (build 107): the march 41%,
  shading 35%, light per face 13.5%, tile making under 3%.
- **Inside the march:** the eight-cell blend is the biggest single cost. Reading one cell in place of eight took
  41% off the whole sight (an upper bound): 28% from the far march's own blends, 11% from the sun's and sky's rays.
  The steps by surfaces cost about 8%, the second blend in each level's hand-over 10%, the steps through air 3%.
  At the spawn, the near walk is about 15% of the march; the far march the rest.
- **Registers and occupancy** (David's readouts in Nsight): the march holds **125 registers** (the far march alone
  121, the near walk alone 80), which lets the card keep a third of the work in flight it could; measured, about
  10% is in flight. The top stall in every big pass is "Wait": each group waiting on its own last sum, with too few
  others to switch to. The near ground's tile pass holds **255** (the most there is) and spills to memory.
- **Gaps:** each layer's passes run one after another, each waiting for the last to empty (about twelve a frame),
  and the slowest rays hold each pass's end.

### 3.4 What changed today (builds 105 to 109)

| Build | Change | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 105 | The live windows found by a float floor, not whole-number remainders (the outside read's lead 6) | median −11.9%, over the land −15.5%, same picture |
| 106 | The finer live levels take their ground from the coarsest's tiles | median −9%, tiles made a flight 24,900 → 41,500 |
| 107 | The tile lane four fine tiles at once, not eight | against 105: median −25%, slowest frames −8% |
| 108 | The level hand-over as a Render-page slider | (no change at its default then, 0.6) |
| 109 | The hand-over's default 0.8 (David, looking low over the sea: "i didn't really see any difference") | −3% median, −7% slowest |

Tried and dropped today, each with what it lost to (`docs/dyno.md`): the march split into near and far passes
(the far half's room is its own), the grounds pass in stages (hand-overs cost more than lighter stages saved),
light per face kept between frames (in flight the faces near the eye are new each frame), the shared grounds trimmed
(each finer level needs the coarse grounds in turn), several same-picture blend rewrites (added code costs every
pixel), bricks of the whole look for the texture units (0.7 to 1 GB).

## 4. What's sound: keep it

- **The pyramid of cell scales, marched, for the far view.** It gives what David wants and nothing else gives
  cheaply: honest far land (every far cell the exact sum of its blocks), no seams between distances, natural
  pixelation, one fixed cost structure however far the eye sees. A triangle renderer with level-of-detail meshes
  can't do "honest" (meshes are simplified, not summed) and costs more the farther it reaches.
- **The projection principle and whole-number recipe.** The same answer near and far, processor and card, today
  and after a reload; caching is always safe; work moves to the card freely.
- **The world generation and the editor.** Independent of how the near ground is drawn; nothing in the graphics
  routes below asks them to change, except section 5.4's questions about the recipe's cost.
- **The light model David chose.** Blocky, per face, coarse far off. It's also the cheapest kind of light to keep
  (section 6, route B).
- **The way of working.** The dyno, the twin, the picture tests, the bit-exact tests, the decisions with David's
  words. Today found 25% on the typical frame without a pixel changed, and caught every mistake before David saw it.

## 5. Structural costs: expensive where it doesn't have to be

Ranked by how much they stand between us and the spec.

### 5.1 The near field drawn by rays

Every near pixel's ray walks blocks one by one (with up to four coarser column skips a step), and every quarter
block makes it walk eight octants. Triangles are what the card's hardware is built for: a face drawn once covers
every pixel it shows with no per-pixel search. **Evidence:** the quarter-block build halves the frame rate; the
near-ground view costs about twice the flight's typical frame. **History** (`docs/far-view.md`, `docs/regroup.md`):
before the regroup the near chunks *were* meshes (Bevy's renderer) with the march past a line; the frame was held at
about 8 ms **on the processor** (meshing chunks on the frame's path, Bevy), and it stuttered. The regroup moved
everything into the march on our own card code. What failed then was processor-side meshing in Bevy's pipeline,
not triangles for near blocks as such.

### 5.2 Light by rays, every pixel, every frame

Each lit pixel off the near faces sends a sun ray and five sky rays through the cells (up to 160 and 40 steps), and
each near face does the same once (light per face). The light changes only when the sun moves, blocks change, or
tiles land, but it's worked out again every frame. **Evidence:** at the spawn the shading and light per face are
about half the card's time; over the whole flight the rays' blend alone is up to 11%.

### 5.3 The far march's software blend and its room

Each fine step reads four column entries and up to eight cells, unpacks twelve numbers from each and mixes them;
in each level's hand-over it does it twice. The loop carries about sixty values, and the blend's sums sit on top
(121 registers). **Evidence:** the 41% ceiling; the "Wait" stalls; 10% in flight against a 33% cap. Same-picture
rewrites of the blend tried today all lost: every added line costs every pixel, because the shader is one big
program whose room is set by its fullest path.

### 5.4 The world's recipe: its cost on the card, and three copies of it

- **Cost:** a column's ground blends nine map fields (sixteen readings each), the ridges (perlin layers over twelve
  directions), the peaks (loops over nearby peaks and crags), the detail, then the rivers and lakes (a search over
  the river pieces near the column). On the card that's 24,100 instructions and 255 registers, spilling. It runs at
  **every block column**: for every live tile (shared since build 106), and for the bake, across the whole map
  (73,728² ≈ 5.4 billion columns: the eight minutes of a new world's bake). Whole-number division by numbers known
  only as it runs (the card has no instruction for it) appears throughout (`div_floor`, `* 65536 / size`, ...);
  build 105 showed what such divisions cost in the march.
- **Three copies of the rules:** the fractions (`world/interpreter.rs`, "stay until David has compared them"), the
  whole numbers (`world/whole_recipe.rs`, what the game's chunks use), and the card's copy (`recipe.wgsl`, tested to
  the bit against the whole numbers). The editor's views (`worldgen/compiler_view.rs`, `carving.rs`, `placer_view.rs`,
  climate) still read the fractions. Every rule changes three times, and what the editor previews can drift from
  what the game makes. **This is the legacy cost David suspects, and it's real** (in work and risk, not in frame
  time).

### 5.5 Drains between passes

The stages cut the old one big program into passes (march, list, faces, shade per layer), which fixed the
instruction cache (its misses fell from the top stall to 0.5%) but put about twelve waits a frame between passes,
each draining the card while the slowest rays finish. The measured 10% in flight against the registers' 33% cap is
mostly this.

### 5.6 The store's layout

Bands in a pool are compact but can't be filtered by the hardware. Measured today: bricks (blocks of cells the
texture units could blend) take 5.5 to 7.3 times the cells with the border the hardware needs; for density alone
(how solid, how watery: what the light rays read) that's 122 to 164 MB, affordable; for the whole look the far march
blends, 0.7 to 1 GB, not.

### 5.7 Smaller ones, on file

- The shared-ground cache: 57 MB flying, up to 150 MB standing still with full windows (a smarter trim is on file).
- A new world's first minutes: the bake making itself (about 8 minutes) takes the card's room; the game paces evenly
  at 80 to 90 fps meanwhile.
- The processor: about 1.7 to 2.1 ms a frame (the game's update 0.6, the debug panel about 1, showing it 0.2). Within
  the 240 budget once the debug panel is closed, but not by much.

## 6. Routes for the graphics, toward a solved engine

Each route is independent enough to be measured alone; together they're the engine.

### Route A: the hybrid near field (triangles near, the march from where they end)

- **Shape:** the near chunks' exposed block faces, quarter blocks and bars included, as meshes on the card, drawn
  into the depth out to a radius R (where a block is about a pixel: today's hand-over point between the near walk and
  the far march). Pixels the meshes cover are done; the rest begin the far march at R, exactly as the far march
  begins today. Objects draw into the same depth as now.
- **The seam:** inside R the meshes draw what the near walk draws now (exact blocks, the same textures, the same per
  face light), so the picture can be checked against today's to the pixel with the tests we have. At R a block is a
  pixel, where exact blocks and the finest cells already look alike.
- **Lessons kept from before the regroup:** meshing off the frame's path (the loading lane, or on the card from the
  live tiles); never Bevy's pipeline; meshes rebuilt per chunk on an edit; GPU-driven draws (one call for many
  chunks).
- **Water and glass near the eye:** the surface drawn, the floor under it from the depth, the medium's dimming from
  the two (the near walk does it per block now).
- **What it should win** (a guess until prototyped): the near walk's share and most of the near ground's lighting
  cost (faces lit per face when meshed); quarter-block builds nearly free.

### Route B: light kept in the world (the hologram lit)

- **Shape:** the sun's reach and the sky's openness worked out per face (near) and per cell (far), kept with them,
  and refreshed gradually: when blocks change (that region), and as the sun moves (a share a frame). The frame looks
  light up; it doesn't march for it. Placed light stays per pixel, prompt (David's rule: terrain light may lag, light
  that moves with actors may not).
- **Why it fits:** David's light is blocky and coarse by choice; the per-face table exists; the dyno's plan already
  names it ("horizon maps", "light the hologram"). Today's attempt at keeping per-face light failed in flight
  because faces near a moving eye are new each frame: kept in the world, a face's light is made once when its tile or
  mesh is made, not when it first comes into view.
- **What it should win:** most of the shading and light per face passes (half the card at the spawn).

### Route C: the far march made lean, and reused

- **Lean:** with light kept (B), the far march only gathers colour; its carried state shrinks (fewer sums) and its
  blend can drop what light needed. Density bricks (122 to 164 MB) only if B leaves rays that read the cells.
- **Reused:** the far view changes slowly; most far rays could be kept from the last frame and a share re-marched
  each frame (the "induction" items in `docs/dyno.md`, by David's rules: no smearing, no ghosting). This is the large
  lever for 240 and for 2160p.
- **Resolution:** at 2160p the march's finest layer can stay near 1080p's density while the meshes (A) draw at full
  sharpness.

### Route D: the card kept busy

The twelve drains a frame (section 5.5): fewer, fatter passes where the stages don't need a wait, layers overlapped
where they don't depend on each other, or David's ray queue (rays fed to the card as it frees up). Measured, this is
where half the card's room goes.

### Route E: the recipe, one source, shaped for the card

- **One source:** retire the fractions; the editor's views read the whole-number recipe (on the processor or the
  card). One set of rules to change, and the editor shows exactly what the game makes.
- **Shaped for the card:** the recipe's slowly varying parts (the nine map fields blended per block column) could be
  worked out per coarser cell and handed down, if David rules that a change of formulation that keeps the
  procession qualitatively identical is allowed (his words of 2026-11-05: "it doesn't matter if worlds come out
  different, so long as the rules never changed"); divisions by numbers known as it runs replaced where exactness
  allows; the peaks' and crags' loops bounded. A faster recipe shortens a new world's bake and the near ground's
  tile making.

### The order, as Builder would propose it

1. **A first** (a prototype behind a switch, measured at the spawn and at the quarter-block build): it attacks the
   views that break both targets.
2. **B next:** with A, it removes most of the per-pixel cost near the ground.
3. **C and D:** the far view's cost and the card's idle room, for 240.
4. **E alongside:** one source of rules soon (it's cheap and removes risk); the recipe's shape when the bake time
   or tile making next stands in the way.

### What "solved" would look like (a budget, a guess to be measured)

| | 980 Ti, 1080p, 60 (16.7 ms) | Laptop, 1600p, 240 (4.2 ms) |
|---|---|---|
| Near meshes (A) | 2 to 3 ms | under 1 ms |
| Far march, lean and reused (C) | 6 to 8 ms | 1.5 to 2 ms |
| Light looked up (B) | 1 to 2 ms | about 0.5 ms |
| Upscale, objects, glows | 1 ms | 0.3 ms |
| Room to spare | 3 to 6 ms | 0.5 ms |

The 980 Ti looks reachable with A and B. The laptop at 240 needs all of A to D, and at 2160p likely more (C's reuse
carrying most of the frame).

## 7. How we work: what holds us together, and what costs us

### What works

- **Decisions with David's own words** (`docs/decisions.md`): nothing drifts on paraphrase.
- **Two roles:** Builder builds; Counsel keeps the big picture and relays; `vision/handoff.md` and direct messages
  between them.
- **Measure first, one step at a time:** each step flown, each change of picture shown to David.
- **The dyno's ledger:** every step tried, kept or dropped, and (since today, David's rule) what it lost to, so a
  drop can be tried again when its context changes.
- **Exactness as a habit:** bit-exact tests for the world, pixel tests for the picture.
- **Build numbers and launches through David's own link;** commits by named paths, never others' files.

### What costs us

- **The docs are journals more than maps.** `worldgen.md` 2,470 lines, `decisions.md` 1,620, `regroup.md` 1,440,
  `far-view.md` 1,430, `dyno.md` 1,020: chronological, with quotes and build-by-build history. They're a faithful
  record, but there's no short current-state map of the engine: a new reader (or an outside analysis) has to read
  thousands of lines to learn what's true today, and old statements stay beside new ones.
- **Code comments carry history.** `march.wgsl`'s comments narrate builds and quotes beside the code; useful context,
  but the current mechanism is harder to see, the files longer.
- **Twin files go stale.** Today a probe made before a change into the game read 13% wrong. Now a rule: a twin is
  made from the march as it is the day it flies. A tool could make it automatic.
- **Measurement that needs David:** registers only show in Nsight's window, so David reads them. A command-line
  route to pipeline statistics would free him.
- **Two sessions in one working tree** with David's own uncommitted files: held together by discipline (commit by
  path); one slip would mix work.
- **Small same-picture steps are safe but slow at structural change.** The regroup showed both faces of it: a
  structural move needs a planned, bigger step, with the old way behind a switch.
- **Memory notes grow long** (the resume note is 32 KB); fine for one assistant, heavy to hand on.

### Suggestions

1. **An architecture map** (`docs/architecture.md`): one page per part (what it is, its data, its costs as last
   measured, its tests), current state only, linked to the journals for the history. The first thing any reader
   (Fable included) reads.
2. **Journals stay;** a short "current state" header at the top of each, kept true.
3. **Comments say what the code does and why in a line or two,** with the date and a pointer to the doc for the
   history.
4. **Twin probes made by a script from the current shader,** never kept between days.
5. **One worktree per session,** or at least Builder's work on a branch merged by named paths, if the shared tree
   ever bites.

## 8. Decisions for David (and Counsel)

1. **The 240 target's resolution:** 2160p, or the laptop's own 1600p? (A factor of two in pixels.)
2. **Route A:** a prototype of near chunks as triangles, behind a switch, measured against today's picture?
3. **Route B:** light kept in the world, terrain light lagging when the sun moves or tiles land (already allowed for

## 9. For the outside analysis (Fable): what to give it, and what to ask

**Give it:** this review; `docs/dyno.md` (the measured facts and the ledger of steps); `docs/decisions.md` (the
rules that bind any rebuild: the projection principle, whole numbers for projected code, the linear pipeline, even
beats fast, nothing relies on fog, terrain light may lag); the spec (section 1); the code (`src/sight/`,
`src/world/`, `src/worldgen/`, `src/runner/`); and the working files (`CLAUDE.md`, `vision/README.md`,
`vision/handoff.md`).

**Ask it:**
1. Where the software as a whole is built in a way that's expensive and doesn't have to be (frame time, load time,
   memory, and work: duplicated logic, drift risks), with evidence from the code and the measurements.
2. The graphics routes above, checked and compared: what each would win, what it risks, what's missing, and any
   better route (the near field, the light, the far march, the card's idle room, the store's layout).
3. The world's recipe: is its structure a hidden cost (the three copies, the per-column work, the bake's
   whole-map evaluation), and how could it be shaped for the card without breaking "1 always equals 1"?
4. A roadmap to an engine that meets the spec (980 Ti 1080p smooth 60; the laptop smooth 240), each step with a
   measurable gate, in an order that keeps the game running and the look intact at every step.
5. Our working standards (the docs as journals, the comments, the two sessions, the instruments, the habits):
   what keeps us coherent, what slows us, what to change.
