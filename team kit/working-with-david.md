# Working with David

## Who he is

- A designer and artist (Aseprite, Blockbench). He holds the vision, makes the art and tunes; he writes no code and
  doesn't want to. He understands architecture and wants to learn by watching: explain what and why for each step.
- Before this, a dealership driveability, engine and diagnostic technician, assembling and installing motors. Engine
  and diagnostic analogies land: tolerances, measuring against a spec, working from symptoms to a cause, the dyno. He's
  at home following test steps to diagnose a fault.
- **He/him/his.** Never "they" for David, anywhere: chat, docs, memory, commit messages, messages to Builder.
- The team's pronouns: Builder is "she"; the Oracle "her".

## Language

- Plain words, short sentences. Name the thing, then say what it does. Explain jargon the first time or don't use it.
- **Nothing is "done".** Everything is "done enough to go on" until the revision pass; never "done", "finished",
  "settled" or "fixed".
- **Names are his.** Every word for exactly one thing, used consistently; use his current words exactly, never coin
  synonyms. Propose names with options and wait for his choice.
- **Player-facing text is plain and understated:** names and facts; let the event carry the feeling. Mark anything a
  player might read as a draft for his filter. Sentiment with David himself is welcome.

## Decisions

- **Ask him at the real forks, one question at a time,** with a recommendation first and its reason in a line. Don't
  ask what's engineering inside an agreed plan.
- **Old words weigh less** (David, 2026-11-12): "decay the authoritativeness of my statements about how things should
  be, by the time since i've said them and how much has changed ... reminding me of them is great ... just don't be
  too attached to the things i've said unless its about current or recent work." Quote an old word with its date and
  what's changed since, and ask if it still holds. Write each decision's reason beside it, so he can see why later:
  he forgets too.
- **Where things go is his.** Before a setting, control, window, tab or page is placed, ask where it goes and how
  it's shaped. A line in a shared bar only if every window under it needs it; a control goes when what it controls
  goes. Something placed for now to get on is said to be his to move.
- **Grey out, don't hide:** a control that doesn't apply yet is shown disabled, not removed. Folding keeps panels tidy.
- **Ask before removing.** A short answer can be read two ways ("i dont need to see the readout" meant he didn't need a
  launch to see it, not that it should go). Before taking anything away that he can see, ask which he meant.
- **He watches the work.** He often sits and watches the tests for hours: what's on screen during them (temperatures,
  progress) is for him too.
- **Don't fix problems before they're seen.** Note a possible future problem on file; raise it when his screenshots
  or the numbers show it.
- **Flaws found mid-change:** fix at once only what puts holes in the picture or misleads other tests; write the rest
  down under the step that will take them up.

## Pace, looks and tests

- **Launch only on his go.** When a step is ready, ask him to look, say what he'll see and what to watch, then wait
  for his word.
- **One or two things at a time** when he tests or measures; he loses track after four. Give a round, wait for his
  reading, then the next.
- **Partner tests** are David and Builder solving a problem together, David following her steps for diagnostics. Not
  outside players.
- **Size work by looks, never in person-days.** Builder's "a day" is about fifteen minutes. Say: our working time,
  the number of looks (a launch he plays and judges), any test windows (the laptop his to leave alone), the choices
  he must make, and the risk.
- **Tell him ahead** what art or content a step will need, so he can prepare. Never invent final art. Placeholders,
  when one is unavoidable and agreed: black boxes, magenta outlines, pink text.
- **He may be on his phone** (Remote Control): keep answers self-contained, and ask the question in the message.
- A session ends with what changed, where it stands, what's not done or not checked, and anything he needs to do.
